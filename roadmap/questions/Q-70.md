# Q-70 — What does R22's mathematics give a target without a float unit?

Asked by R22.14 on 2026-11-20, in the rewrite of R22.

Cortex-M0, M0+ and M3 and the RV32 boards without the F extension compute
`f32` and `f64` through libgcc's soft float; an M4F has a unit for `f32`
only. Every routine of R22 comes in `f32` and `f64` on every target, and
reachability keeps the precision a program does not use out of its image,
so a firmware program already gets the whole library in floating point,
at soft float's cost. Firmware signal processing on such parts uses fixed
point instead, the Q15 and Q31 formats CMSIS-DSP offers, which is several
times faster than soft float there. R22.14 is written as that fixed-point
item, and R22.73 gives its transforms and filters in Q15 and Q31 through
it.

**Options:**

1. `f32` and `f64` through soft float only; R22.14 is removed and R22.73
   loses its fixed-point forms.
2. `f32` and `f64` everywhere, and R22.14's Q15 and Q31 arithmetic,
   elementary functions, dot products and filters, with R22.73's
   transforms in fixed point.
3. Only `f32` in the library on a target without a double-precision unit.

**Recommendation:** option 2. The range from a 32 KB microcontroller up is
Landin's own, and fixed point is what a firmware author on those parts
would otherwise take from C.

**Default until answered:** `f32` and `f64` on every target through soft
float where there is no unit, and R22.14's fixed-point item kept as
written, with R22.73's Q15 and Q31 forms.

**What waits on the answer:** R22.14, and R22.73's dependency on it.

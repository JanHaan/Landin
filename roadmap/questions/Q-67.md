# Q-67 — Are matrices of complex numbers in R22's linear algebra?

Asked by R22.15 on 2026-11-20, in the rewrite of R22.

R22.11 gives a complex number type with its arithmetic and functions;
R22.17 returns a real matrix's complex eigenvalues in it, R22.70 a
polynomial's complex roots and R22.73 the Fourier transforms. Matrices of
complex elements are another matter: every routine of R22.15 to R22.17
again for complex elements, with Hermitian in place of symmetric. BLAS
and LAPACK carry both, as their `c` and `z` routines; quantum mechanics,
electromagnetics and control use them, most other fields do not.

**Options:**

1. Real matrices only in R22.15 to R22.19; complex scalars, eigenvalues
   and transforms as written; complex matrices named as left out.
2. Complex matrices in R22.15 to R22.17 as well, Hermitian forms included.
3. Real matrices in R22.15 to R22.19, and complex matrices as a later item
   of R22 after R22.17.

**Recommendation:** option 1. It halves the dense linear algebra's
routines; whether a routine is written once over a concept that complex
numbers could later meet is R20's answer, which keeps option 3 open.

**Default until answered:** real matrices only, complex scalars,
eigenvalues and transforms as written, and complex matrices named as left
out in R22.15 to R22.17.

**What waits on the answer:** the scope of R22.15, R22.16 and R22.17.

**Answer (2026-11-20, the maintainer):** option 1 for now: real matrices
only in R22.15 to R22.19, with complex scalars, eigenvalues and
transforms as written. Complex matrices are kept in mind as a later
addition, and R22.15's matrix design leaves room for them. Closed.

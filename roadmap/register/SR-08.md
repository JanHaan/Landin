activation: A real routine needs more than 16,384 declarations, or measured origin storage for a routine within the bound is unacceptable on a supported host.

D247's 16,384-declaration routine bound limits the measured cost of origin tracking. Since R13.78 the reference pass's derivation facts are sparse, each naming the locals it derives from, and branches and loops share unchanged facts; what remains is the host stack at larger routines, below. The separate 16,384-field struct bound has a different cause. Measured again in R13.61: `scaling.sh`'s locals family peaks in checking, which grows from 61 to 141 MiB and from 0.10 to 0.28 s of processor time between 8,000 and 16,000 locals, so its peak-memory ratio was 2.45 against the 2.5 bound, its 16,000 point unchanged since R13.60. Met again in R13.70: the locals family's frontend grew 2.52 times from 8,000 to 16,000 and failed the gate's scaling job, the reference pass's joins ORing every derivation byte; a join now reads only the bytes between a fact's lowest and highest local, which took the checking stage at 16,000 locals from 0.27 to 0.20 s, but copying and comparing a fact still costs the routine's size. Answered in R13.78: the reference pass's derivation facts are sparse, each naming the locals it derives from, after the candidate's gate failed `scaling` at 2.80x on `locals` and 3.05x on `branches`; checking's exact instructions now grow 1.89x and 1.95x per doubling to 16,000 locals, and 1.94x and 1.99x to 64,000 on a compiler built with the bound raised, its peak from 78 to 291 MiB. A `branches`-shaped routine of 64,000 locals exhausts the default 8 MiB host stack, "refine: host resources exhausted", so a higher bound needs that recursion found first.

Completion: Raise or remove the routine bound only to the extent the measurements support, after the stack depth at large routines is bounded, updating D247 and its size-bound test; leave the struct field bound independently justified.

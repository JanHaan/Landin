# Code of conduct

Landin is discussed in public, mostly by people who have never met. This
page says what everyone taking part can expect of everyone else, the
maintainer included, and what happens when that goes wrong. It applies
wherever the project hosts a discussion, and to anyone who speaks for the
project elsewhere.

## What is expected

- Argue with the idea, not with the person. Landin's design has been argued
  over hard, and its record of rejected ideas exists because people could
  be wrong in public without paying for it.
- Be specific. A refusal that told you nothing, with the command and its
  output, is something to act on; a verdict on the whole compiler is not.
- Not sure what would help? Say so and ask; nobody is expected to arrive
  knowing how to reduce a program to a report.
- Assume the other person is competent and means well.
- Many people here write English as a second language, and tone in text
  is easy to misread. Read generously and write plainly.
- Respect people's time: search before asking, and cut a report down to
  what shows the problem.

## What is not tolerated

- Harassment, insults, slurs, or attacks on someone's identity or
  background.
- Sexual language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Threats, and publishing someone's private information.
- Spam, and sustained disruption of a discussion.

## Reporting

Every post on GitHub has a menu with **Report content**. Choose **Report to
repository admins** and the report reaches the maintainer privately; the
person reported is not told who reported them. To report the maintainer, or
anything a post report cannot carry, choose **Report to GitHub** from the
same menu instead.

## What happens

The maintainer reads every report and answers it in proportion, from a
note asking for a change, through editing or hiding a post or locking a
discussion, to blocking someone from the repository for a while or for
good. Plainly malicious behaviour, such as spam or a threat, may go straight
to a block. Whoever reported it hears the outcome, as far as telling them
exposes nobody else.

This wording is the project's own, and draws on the
[Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/).
